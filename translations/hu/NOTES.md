# Hungarian edition — decisions and glossary (NOTES.md)

Fixed once, held for the whole book (TRANSLATION-PROMPT §4–§5, §9). English for the editors, Hungarian renderings. Standard: prose that reads as written in Hungarian; only names, places and the deliberately foreign words (§3) may read as imported. The native reader's earlier note on the probe — „einige ungarische Redewendungen/Gewohnheiten sind nicht 100% passend“ — is the reason the calque list in `NATIVE-SHEET.md` exists; every marked passage they send goes into it.

## A. Title and closing line — one decision

**Title: „Milyen érzés az eső“.** *Milyen érzés …* is the Hungarian for „what does it feel like“, and the phrase survives every site it must occupy:

- Ch55, the last words of the book, fixed verbatim:
  > – Tudom, hogy látsz – mondja. – Hát figyelj. Ilyen érzés az eső…
- Ch38, ALEPH's letter, fixed verbatim: **Nem tudom, milyen érzés az eső!**

Rejected: „Milyen az eső“ (drops *feel*), „Amilyennek az eső érződik“ (bookish), „Mit érez az eső“ (wrong subject). Casual English *felt like* (= seemed) is rendered *úgy tűnt / olyan volt, mint*, never with *érzés*.

## C. The reserved phrase

> Túl tökéletes. Túl – hatékony.

Ch22: „Túl tökéletes, gondoltam. Túl – hatékony.“ Ch47, italic inside her speech: „Azt gondoltam: *Túl tökéletes. Túl – hatékony.*“ The spaced en dash; *hatékony* the last word both times.

## G. Typography — magyar helyesírás

- Dialogue: **gondolatjel (–, U+2013) at line start**, space after; inquit and interpolation set off by spaced dashes: „– Ugyanaz a minta – mondja halkan. – Más koordináták.“ No quotation marks for direct speech.
- Quotation marks „…” (U+201E … U+201D) only for quoted words inside narration; inner »…«.
- Parenthetical dash: spaced en dash „ – “. Never an em dash.
- Ellipsis „…“; the book's last words end in „…“.
- Times „6:47“ for ALEPH's timestamps, „negyed nyolckor“ in prose [probe]; dates „2031 novemberében“; „Berlin 2031-ben“.
- Numbers under thirteen in words in prose; „1400“ (no separator below five digits), decimals „0,5“.
- Italics `*…*` exactly where the English has them; ch53 keeps its `#`/`###`/`##` headings; chat lines italic, no dash.
