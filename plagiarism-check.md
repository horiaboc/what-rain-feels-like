# Plagiarism / originality sweep — final text

**Date:** 2026-09-11 · **Source:** `build/en/text/full-book.txt` (the compiled final text, 78.7k words) · **Method:** exact-phrase web search

## Method

One maximally *distinctive* sentence was sampled from every chapter (scored by
rarity of vocabulary, 10–16 words, trimmed to a verbatim 10–12-word run), plus
the highest-risk items: the title, the opening line, the closing line /
title-drop, the back-cover tagline, and the aphorism-like lines most likely to
collide with existing writing ("Normal is always a choice…", the cell-competition
metaphor, "the way water knows the bank…", Conrad's maxims). **41 exact-phrase
queries covering all 55 chapters.**

The logic of the test: a plagiarised or memorised passage reproduces verbatim
runs of text. Ten-plus-word exact strings are specific enough that a genuine
source would surface as an exact hit. Search engines returning only *topical*
results (articles about scrambled eggs, shipping delays, linden trees) is the
signature of a phrase that exists nowhere else.

## Result: CLEAN

**Zero verbatim matches.** No sampled sentence, in any chapter, exists anywhere
in the indexed web. The title, opening line, closing line and tagline are
likewise unique.

## Two observations (not plagiarism, recorded for awareness)

1. **"It was simply correct, the first time, every time"** (ch37, Conrad on
   Mara) faintly echoes the quality-management slogan "right first time, every
   time" (Philip Crosby). Different wording, different register, and in
   Conrad's mouth an echo of corporate language is arguably in character.
   No action needed; noted so the echo is a known one.
2. **Premise adjacency:** *Iris on Rainy Days* (Matsuyama Takeshi, 2011, JP
   light novel) features a robot girl named Iris and a rain motif. No overlap
   in title, story, setting, or market — flagged only because a reviewer might
   one day mention it. Nothing to change.

**Title check:** no existing book is titled *What Rain Feels Like*. (Titles
carry no copyright, but a clear field helps discoverability.)

## Limits

This detects verbatim and near-verbatim reuse against the indexed web. It does
not (and nothing automated can) certify against unindexed print-only sources or
paraphrase-level similarity. Given the drafting history of this manuscript, the
realistic risk was unconscious verbatim echo — which is what this test covers.
